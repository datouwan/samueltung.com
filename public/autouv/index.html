Learn where to cut.
Let the solver flatten.

autouv is an automatic UV unwrapper aimed at AI-generated 3D models. A neural network predicts the seams an artist would place; classical parameterization and packing do the rest. The target is a layout an artist can paint on: a handful of large islands, not hundreds of fragments.

Goal

Few, large, paintable islands.

Every existing automatic unwrapper fails the same way. It optimizes distortion, and the cheapest way to lower distortion is to cut more. Artists accept stretch in exchange for charts they can work with.

Not the goal

Fragmented atlas

xatlas, Blender Smart UV and most learned methods return dozens to hundreds of small islands. Distortion is low. Nobody can hand-paint on it, texture bleeding shows at every boundary, and downstream tools choke on the seam count.

The goal

Artist layout

A handful of charts that follow the shape. Seams hidden in creases and on the back side. Some stretch tolerated. Loop cuts that double as part decomposition. This is what MeshTailor's results look like and why artists reacted to it.

Objective

minimize chart count + boundary jaggedness subject to distortion ≤ ceiling

Distortion is a constraint, not the target. Chart count and boundary regularity are the headline metrics and, in the self-supervised phase, the reward.

Primary use case: generated meshes

Outputs from Hunyuan3D, Trellis, Meshy, Tripo and similar arrive as dense marching-cubes triangle soup, often 100k to 500k faces, noisy at every vertex, with no edge flow. UV layout is the biggest remaining pain in that pipeline, and the place autouv has to win for GameLoop.

Train on the best artists, not the average

Accepted samples are ranked by charts per unit of surface area and seam length, and training is weighted toward the low-fragmentation end. Imitating the median Sketchfab upload produces a median unwrap.

Thesis

Only one of the three steps needs learning.

Unwrapping is cutting, flattening, and packing. Two of those are solved by deterministic geometry. The third is a discrete, taste-driven decision, and that is exactly where automatic tools fall short of an artist.

Learned

Seam placement

Which edges to cut so charts are few, low-distortion, and follow the shape the way a human would. Combinatorial, and artists disagree with every heuristic in xatlas and Blender.

Solver

Flattening

Given a chart that is a topological disk, ABF++, LSCM, SLIM and BFF flatten it with near-optimal distortion. No reason to learn coordinates.

Solver

Packing

Placing charts in the atlas is a bin-packing problem with good existing tools. xatlas packs charts once seams are decided.

Labels are free

Merge UV-split vertices by position, then an edge is a seam when its two faces disagree about the UV coordinates of its endpoints. Chart ids follow as connected components across non-seam edges. No human labeling.

Never learn coordinates end to end

Nuvo (2023) learned UV maps directly and produced artist-unfriendly charts. Every method that has looked right to artists since, SeamGPT, PartUV, MeshTailor, learns the cut and hands flattening to a solver.

What it does

  • Learns seams only; flattens with ABF++
  • Labels from existing UV islands, the same extraction we use
  • Data: ~300k part-level samples from TexVerse + 110k garments from GarmentCodeData
  • Dual-stream encoder: graph encoder on mesh connectivity + frozen Michelangelo point-cloud encoder, fused by cross-attention
  • Autoregressive pointer decoder traces seams vertex by vertex, coarse-to-fine chain ordering

Where it stops

  • Training meshes capped at 2,000 triangles and 400 seam tokens; larger meshes need decimation or divide-and-conquer
  • Seams degrade under small vertex noise (σ ≈ 0.01), exactly the regime of generated meshes
  • Failures reported on hair, spiky geometry, very low-poly meshes
  • 8× RTX 4090 for 30 epochs; held-out set drawn from the training distributions

Implication: the seam model is a known quantity. The open problem is applying it to dense, noisy generator output. That is where autouv focuses.

What works

  • At threshold 0.85 the model lands on the artist's chart count, 45.5, with the same seam length, seams just as smooth, and stretch 1.04 against the artist's 1.09. xatlas needs 158 charts. On 47% of meshes the model uses fewer charts than the artist; on 41% it does so with stretch under 1.3.
  • The stretch problem was solved at the flattener, not by cutting more: SLIM after LSCM, with a Tutte embedding as fallback when LSCM collapses, plus a safety ceiling that cuts one chart per round.
  • Our flattener beats the stored artist layouts on distortion (p90 1.07 vs 1.25), so the comparison is not flattering the model.
  • Cleaning the data (per-material overlap, tiers, deduplication) moved the seam classifier from 0.45 to 0.61 F1 on held-out meshes.

What does not, yet

  • Parity is not victory. Yesterday's "35 charts vs 45" was an artifact of under-cut charts. With stretch fixed, the model equals the artist's count rather than beating it. Fewer charts than the artist at artist-level stretch is the next model's job, not the pipeline's.
  • Cut placement. The model cuts in plausible places but not always where an artist would: on the bottle below it merges body and cap into one curved chart instead of one straight rectangular label. Fewer charts is necessary, not sufficient.
  • Packing. Coverage 0.44 vs the artist's 0.55 and xatlas's 0.64; our charts pack less tightly than xatlas's own, even through xatlas's packer.
  • Prop sets. Meshes made of many separate parts (the crime-scene set below) still fragment and stretch; per-part handling is needed.
  • Tier A is too lenient. Scans with hundreds of auto-generated islands still slip in; human ratings in the viewer will recalibrate the ranker.

Reading it

  • Faces and organic shapes are where the model is strongest: fewer charts than the artist at lower stretch, winning on half the meshes.
  • Characters and animals are near parity, vehicles are behind. Hard-surface assets with many rigid parts want one chart per part, which per-edge voting does not produce.
  • xatlas needs 4 to 5 times the charts in every category. The learned seams are doing the work; the pipeline just carries them.

What the pictures also say

  • The model's favourite move is the pelt: one big island for a body or a head plus a few small ones. Low chart count, low stretch, and a layout many painters would still cut further. Chart count is a proxy; the ratings in the viewer will tell us where the pelt is welcome.
  • Some "artist" layouts in tier A are themselves fragmented (55 charts for a cartoon head). The ranker still needs human calibration.

Pipeline

Dense mesh in, paintable UVs out.

Repair triangulates, removes non-manifold edges, fills pinholes and drops floating fragments. The proxy is a remeshed or decimated 2k to 5k triangle version with a map back to the dense mesh. Cut graph cleanup thresholds, joins nearby segments, prunes stubs and adds cuts until every chart is a disk under the distortion ceiling. Projection lifts proxy seams onto the dense mesh as geodesic paths and snaps them to edges. Flattening is ABF++ or SLIM, packing is xatlas. The engineering stages get as much budget as the model.

License filter

Keep CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-SA. Drop CC-BY-NC and CC-BY-ND for anything that ships commercially. License, author, and source URL are stored with every sample.

Quality filter

  • 500 to 100k faces, near-manifold
  • Seam ratio in a plausible band: rejects untouched meshes and per-face shreds
  • Area-weighted conformal distortion: median under 1.5, p90 under 3
  • Few folded or degenerate UV triangles
  • Charts inside one tile after per-chart normalization
  • Mirrored islands and overlap under 5% for v1

Sample format

One compressed npz per mesh: vertices, faces, UVs, geometric vertex ids, edge list, per-edge seam label, per-face chart id. A JSON sidecar holds metrics, reject reasons, and provenance.

Model

A cheap baseline, a MeshTailor-class target, then refinement.

The baseline exists to be measured against. The autoregressive model is what produces coherent, few-chart layouts. Refinement runs on the distribution no artist dataset covers.

Baseline

Per-edge seam classifier

  • DiffusionNet or point transformer, features projected to edges
  • Weighted BCE plus dice; seams are 2 to 5% of edges
  • 10 to 50M parameters, a day on one GPU
  • Easy to verify against artist seams; tends to fragment, which is the point of measuring it
Main target

Autoregressive seam generation

  • Graph encoder on the proxy mesh plus a frozen point-cloud encoder
  • Pointer decoder emits seam chains vertex by vertex
  • Coarse-to-fine ordering: global structural cuts first, local detail last
  • Trained with vertex-noise augmentation for generator robustness
  • Multi-GPU training on a RunPod cluster
Refinement

Self-supervised on generator output

Fine-tune from the main model with a reward built from chart count, boundary jaggedness, seam visibility, and distortion under the ceiling. Needs no labels, so it runs directly on Hunyuan3D, Trellis, Meshy and Tripo outputs, the exact distribution we care about.

Metrics

Headline, constraint, and the final arbiter.

Baselines are xatlas, Blender Smart UV, and MeshTailor if its code lands. Every unwrap is scored the same way, artist or machine.

Roadmap

Phases

Status is updated here as phases close. The repo holds the detailed plan.

PHASE 0

Baseline and metrics

Done 2026-09-01. Mesh loader that preserves artist UVs, seam and chart extraction, distortion and atlas metrics, quality filters that tell folds from mirrored islands, xatlas baseline, tests. Smoke-tested on Khronos sample assets.

PHASE 1

Dataset

Done 2026-09-02, first cut. 30k Objaverse objects harvested, per-material metrics, quality tiers, deduplication: 8,589 accepted meshes, 6,357 tier A. Still to come: TexVerse, GarmentCodeData, Amazon Berkeley Objects as a gold validation set, and human ratings to recalibrate the tiers.

PHASE 2

Baseline seam classifier

Done 2026-09-02. 1.9M-parameter edge-graph network, test F1 0.614 and AP 0.661 at an 11% seam base rate. The yardstick exists; the architecture is near its ceiling.

PHASE 3

Seams to layouts, then the autoregressive model

In progress. The seam-to-UV pipeline (cut, repair topology, LSCM, pack) is built and evaluated: 35 charts vs the artist's 45 at the cost of area stretch on under-cut charts. Next: a distortion ceiling that keeps cutting until every chart is under it, a real packer, then the MeshTailor-class autoregressive seam model trained on the phase 1 set with noise augmentation.

PHASE 4

Proxy and projection

Repair, remeshing to proxy, geodesic seam lifting back to the dense mesh, flatten and pack. End-to-end unwrap of raw generator output.

PHASE 5

Self-supervised refinement

Reward from chart count, jaggedness, seam visibility and distortion, on unlabeled generator outputs. Artist preference study as the gate.

PHASE 6

Deployment

RunPod serverless endpoint: glTF or OBJ in, mesh with UVs out. Blender and Godot importer hooks for GameLoop.

Risks

Known ways this goes wrong.

Noise sensitivity

Generated meshes are noisy everywhere and MeshTailor degrades at σ ≈ 0.01. The proxy step must denoise, and training must augment with vertex noise.

Projection quality

Lifting seams from the proxy to the dense mesh can produce jagged or wandering cuts. Geodesic tracing and edge snapping need their own tests and metrics.

Artist styles conflict

Hard-surface and organic assets get unwrapped differently. Either condition on an asset category or accept a blended style.

Mirrored UVs

Symmetric assets overlap mirrored halves on purpose; DamagedHelmet and Duck both do. Excluded in v1, possible separate label class later.

Compute

The autoregressive model needs multi-GPU training. Budget a RunPod cluster for phase 3, not a single card.

The model is not the product

A seam model alone never unwraps anything. Cut graph cleanup, projection and solver integration get as much time as training.
