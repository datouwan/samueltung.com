Competitive assessment · 2026-08-14

The incumbent, measured.
PlayScore 2 / ReadScoreLib.

PlayScore 2 is the market's benchmark for "point a phone at a score and hear it play." This note explains — from public sources only — why it feels instant, why it feels highly accurate, where those impressions are real versus perceptual, and a calibrated answer to the question that matters: can this track beat it for a product where the sheet is rendered as notation and corrected WYSIWYG by a teacher who then shares it with students?

Why it feels instant

Cheap pipeline, streaming architecture, playback-first UX.

All three reasons are confirmed in primary sources — the 2021 Dorico interview with creator Anthony Wilkes, the SeeScore developer pages, and Scoring Notes' reviews.

Compiled C library, on-device

ReadScoreLib is a native embedded SDK — no cloud round-trip, no large autoregressive decode. Classical compiler-style stages (thresholding, staff finding, symbol classification, parsing) are computationally cheap. Wilkes quotes ~1 page/second, improved to 3–4 pages/second after parallelizing page processing.

Per-page front end, score-wide back end

Early recognition runs page by page; only key/time analysis and cross-page relationships (ties, slurs) need the whole score. Playback can start the moment page one is parsed — the app feels instant by architecture, even on long documents.

Playback-first product design

Text and lyrics recognition is off by default to maximize speed; audio starts immediately after capture. A large share of "instant" is latency-hiding UX, not raw throughput.

Why it feels accurate

Half real engineering, half a forgiving metric.

Users report "98% accuracy." Head-to-head reviews rank it mid-tier for notation fidelity. Both are true — they measure different things.

Real: a grammar layer amplifies raw recognition

Syntax-directed parsing, semantic analysis, and score-wide statistical reconciliation repair visual-layer errors: measures must sum to the time signature, an obscured clef can be deduced from the key signature, missing elements get reconstructed. Musical constraints act as an accuracy multiplier over whatever the symbol classifier alone achieves. This is the genuinely borrowable idea.

Perceptual: playback hides most error classes

Scoring Notes' comparison ranked PlayScore mid-tier for MusicXML fidelity — behind the ML-based Newzik and Soundslice — and found it drops cue notes, tempo and dynamic markings, repeats, and measure numbers. None of that is audible when a simple piece plays back. Judged by ear, only pitch and rhythm count — exactly where the grammar layer is strongest. The forgiving metric flatters it.

Calibrated confidence

Can this track beat it?

Honest answer: yes on the declared scope and on the teacher workflow — with one known risk and one deliberately conceded frontier.

High confidence

Within V1 scope (single staff, monophonic)

The A5 evidence: TER 0.0632 on real photos, 29/60 exact systems, all 137 printable accidentals recovered — from a 6M-parameter CRNN trained ~25 minutes on synthetic renders. The structural bet is the same one ReadScoreLib validates (recognize the ink, derive the semantics in code), but the deterministic engraving state machine makes pitch-spelling errors impossible by construction instead of repairing them afterward.

High confidence

On the teacher workflow as a whole

PlayScore has no editor; correction means export → MuseScore → manual cleanup, with no link back to the photo. This track's graphic-first IR is built for a correction UI:

  • CTC yields per-token confidence free — tint the few uncertain measures instead of proofreading all of them;
  • object coordinates — click a rendered note, see the original ink beside it; correction becomes verification;
  • Verovio (already the render harness) is the right rights-clean WYSIWYG engine: SVG with element IDs mapping back to the source encoding.

The product metric is minutes-to-correct-sheet, not raw TER. A 95%-accurate system with confidence highlighting beats a 98%-accurate black box that emits MusicXML.

Tempered

On general repertoire

The gap to PlayScore is scope, not per-symbol quality: chords, multiple voices, cross-staff beaming, grace notes, tuplets, multi-page continuity — decades of engineering. Matching that breadth is a multi-year effort. But teacher practice material (etudes, exercises, method-book pieces) skews toward single or two staves with light polyphony — chords plus full-page reading (the A7+ items, in that order) likely cover most of it.

Known risk

Full-page reading on real photos

All current numbers are per-system crops; the page → systems splitter has not met real photos yet. This is where PlayScore's geometric-normalization stage earns its keep. Engineering risk rather than research risk — but it sits between the current results and a usable product, and deserves its own gate.

Actions

Three decisions this assessment locks in.

Recorded in docs/PLAYSCORE_ASSESSMENT.md in the repo; this page is the readable mirror.

1

Benchmark the incumbent, rights-clean

Once the B1 photos are captured, run the same 30 pages through PlayScore 2, export its MusicXML, and score it with the identical TER metric against the same references. The benchmark content is generated in-house, so this is clean — and it replaces estimation with a sealed, apples-to-apples number.

2

Promote confidence + coordinates to first-class IR

Per-token confidence and recognized-object coordinates must survive into the Score IR — the WYSIWYG editor depends on both. They stop being debug artifacts and become product requirements.

3

Confirm the roadmap ordering

The A7+ sequence — chords/double stops first, then full-page multi-system reading — is exactly the critical path for the teacher persona. No reordering needed; this assessment validates it.
