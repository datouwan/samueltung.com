07

Risks and validation priorities

The market thesis depends on measurable product proof.

Scale distribution only after the correction burden, audio reliability, feedback quality, retention, teacher adoption, and privacy model are credible.

RiskQuestionValidation
OMR reliabilityCan ordinary families scan assigned music with a low correction burden?Measure the percentage of measures requiring zero correction.
Audio assessmentCan the app evaluate real instruments in home environments?Test violin, piano, and one wind instrument across phones and rooms.
Feedback qualityDoes feedback help instead of distracting or contradicting the teacher?Co-design feedback language with private teachers.
RetentionDoes a weekly habit survive after scanning novelty fades?Track successful practice days and 8–12 week retention.
Teacher adoptionWill teachers recommend it without feeling displaced?Position it as opt-in between-lesson support.
Copyright and privacyCan user-owned scores and child audio be handled safely?Local-first storage where practical and clear parental controls.

Market conclusion

A credible niche can become an enduring business.

The initial U.S. serviceable segment is roughly 1.2M–1.8M notation-centric privately taught students. The disciplined path is to prove reliable score capture and useful between-lesson feedback before scaling teacher-led distribution.

$8M–$10M ARRPotential at approximately 100,000 paying families, before school, studio, or international channels.
